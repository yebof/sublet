## 3. The policies

The pre-registration lists every policy with all of its parameters. The tables below are the output of `python -m sublet.experiments.ms5b_prereg_tags` at the freeze commit, run from `code/sim` (module `experiments/ms5b_prereg_tags.py`; its docstring says what it prints). A tag that a week, gap, E5, E3 or Q44 job runs is built on the confirmation's first week instance, `bench_e7f` config seed 301 (lender seed 301), under L-tr with policy seed 0, as `e7h.run_week` builds it. A tag that only the fam part runs is built on the first instance of the fam job list, as `e7h.run_fam` builds it. A tag that both kinds of job run is built on both instances, and its resolved parameters are equal on both. The resolved parameters are read as `e7.run_entry` records them, {**tag parameters, **policy.params()}. Where `params()` leaves out a key that holds its default (the baselines' `QUIET_KEYS`), the class's `PARAMS` default is filled in, and the key is listed in the column "filled from PARAMS".

## 6. Definitions

The definitions of the note's §4.5 (endpoints, revocation cost, stress-intensity curve, statistics with the bootstrap seed 20261031 and 10,000 resamples, dependence, missing runs), copied verbatim from the note at the anchor, lines 244–254:

Per week $w$ and policy $p$: $r_0(w,p)$ under L-tr and $W_0(w,p)=\max$ of $r_0$ over the five stress modes.

*Revocation cost* (D6). $\mathrm{RC}(w,p,M)=r_0(w,p,M)-r_0(w,p,\text{L-hon})$, same week, policy and policy seed, where L-hon is the lender model `{"schedule": "iid_step", "params": {"D_slots": 1, "p": 0.0}}` (defined in `ms5b_common`): no announcement, no refusal for unavailability and no buffer violation (`lender.REFUSE_SCHEDULES` and `VIOLATION_SCHEDULES` hold only `trace` and `correlated`), so every lease lives until the platform releases it or the horizon ends; capacities are unchanged. RC is what the lender's announcements, refusals and violations add to the policy's bill, its reactions included; under L-tr it includes the refusals of purchases in announced bands. It can be negative when the policy's path under L-hon is worse; it is reported as measured. Table III reports RC under L-tr; the supplement reports RC under every mode, with each run's fallback and stranding totals beside it and MS4's fallback share after reclaims for continuity. P0 checks the definition on development-exposed cells (§5).

*Stress-intensity curve:* $r_0$ against the `iid_step` $p$ grid of (a) and (b) ($p=0$ is L-hon), with each policy's first $p$ at which $r_0>1$.

*Statistics:* per-week paired differences $\Delta_w=r_0(w,\text{best})-r_0(w,\text{Sublet})$, with best the per-week minimum over the comparison set; a one-sided sign test (12 of 16 gives $p=2517/65536=0.0384$, derived); the median of $\Delta_w$ with a week-bootstrap 95% percentile interval (10,000 resamples of the 16 weeks, `numpy.random.default_rng(20261031)`); the one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test reported.

*Dependence:* the weeks are not independent samples. Every MaaS lender's availability is read from one one-week series (`code/analysis/out/a2_slack_series.npz`, 2,016 five-minute slots per cluster) at an offset drawn per config seed modulo its length (`config._resolve_random`, `instance.py`), and the demand from fixed WildChat sources at drawn offsets, so a week covers the same availability and demand series as every other week, rotated, and development studied these series (§4.1). The sign test, the bootstrap interval and the Wilcoxon test are therefore descriptive: the paper says so, gives them as summaries of the 16 weeks, and shows every week's result, not only the pooled tests.

*Missing runs:* a run that crashes, is killed for memory or time, or has an invariant failure is rerun once with the same seeds and code. If it fails again, the week counts as a loss for Sublet in every hypothesis that uses that run, whichever policy's run is missing, and the failure is reported.

§4.5 at the freeze is the same as at the anchor: the note's §4–§7 are unchanged between the anchor and the freeze commit. There is no deviation in §4.5.

## 8. The gates

- *Start of the confirmation.* Every confirmation part of `e7h.py` (main, seeds, second, e3, fam, grid) calls `e7h.gate`, which calls `ms5b_common.check_confirmation`. The gate refuses the part unless three conditions hold: this file exists at HEAD; the working tree is clean (`ms5b_common.dirty_paths`: no tracked change and no untracked path outside `code/sim/out/`); and the sha256 of `experiments/ms5b_analysis.py`, on disk and at HEAD, equals the `analysis_sha256` recorded above. `python -m sublet.experiments.e7h --check-start` applies the gate of the parts in `E7H_PART`, and `run_e7h.sh` runs it before each part and, when it is refused, exits with status 2, so neither that part nor any later part of `E7H_PART` starts. Reduced runs record the gate without enforcing it; they use development cells only.
- *Before the first confirmation run.* Proposition P, Remark R and their audit are complete (§7, C1).
- *Missing runs.* A run that crashes, is killed for memory or time, or has an invariant failure is rerun once with the same seeds and code (`e7h --retry-list` and `run_e7h.sh`'s retry pass); the missing-run rule of §6 then applies.
- *Copy.* The outputs are copied from the clean clone into `code/sim/out/sublet_v2/` and checked with `python -m sublet.experiments.ms5b_common --check-copy CLONE` (record `out/ms3_copy_sublet_v2.json`). Every number of the confirmation comes from a versioned output under `code/sim/out/sublet_v2/` (the note's §6).
- *After the freeze.* Any code change voids the confirmation set. A new round then uses fresh seeds (`bench_e7f` config seeds 317–332) and is reported as a second round. A failed criterion is reported as measured, with no retuning.
